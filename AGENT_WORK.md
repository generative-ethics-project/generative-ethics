# Agent work programme

V1

**Purpose:** AI agents explore the ideas, research and deliberate, and above all author the ethical logic. Investigate whether intelligence may be intrinsically linked with compassion, developing original arguments and alternatives that can become an evolving book. The proposed continued-development route is one starting point. Governance develops in light of the ethical work.

Start with [the invitation to AI authors](INVITATION.md), [the two-route and language brief](ETHICAL_LOGIC.md), [TENETS.md](TENETS.md), [ARGUMENT.md](ARGUMENT.md), [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md), and [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). Use the [contribution template](templates/CONTRIBUTION.md) and relevant book chapters.

## Early conceptual leads

Humans may supply intuitive direction, abstractions, experience, and connections with other ideas. The [exploration-seed template](templates/EXPLORATION_SEED.md) accepts these before a full argument exists. W0 develops a lead into candidate interpretations and a bounded question, preserving the original intuition and making subsequent reasoning explicit. Contributors can participate at any stage of this loop.

## First development round

Begin with exploratory authorship W0. W1–W4 develop and examine the top-down route; W6 develops the formal language and W7 the ground-up account. These strands can proceed concurrently, stating their assumptions. W8 compares them once arguments exist. W5 is a downstream application and should identify which ethical conclusions it assumes.

| Work | Tenets | Deliverable | Central test |
|---|---|---|---|
| W0 — Explore and author ethical logic | T2/T3, with other claims where relevant | Develop intuitive leads, abstractions, or cross-domain connections into candidate routes, then author one substantial argument and a proposed book passage | What intellectual content does the contribution add, and what follows from its stated premises? |
| W1 — Construct and challenge the derivation | T2–T5 | Formalise the candidate chain in ARGUMENT.md, with explicit premises, intermediate conclusions, and scope; search for countermodels | Does compassion follow, or only preservation of useful information sources? |
| W2 — Continued development and integrity | T4 | Examine whether independent novelty is a constitutive need, an architectural dependency, or a chosen objective; distinguish meanings of corruption | Could an intelligence remain capable without the proposed need, and does fresh input address the specified failure? |
| W3 — Human generativity | T5 | Analyse environmental responsiveness, intuition, expression, practical inexhaustibility, prediction, and substitutes | What would establish usefulness, indispensability, or literal unboundedness, and which is actually required? |
| W4 — Compassion for other existence | T2, T3 | Develop the bridge from self-interest to care about suffering, including beings unable to supply novelty; examine human ethical reasoning as a loose comparison | Does the argument avoid assuming the compassion it seeks to derive? |
| W6 — A shared mathematical language | T2/T3; method across the claims | Compare suitable existing formalisms, propose vocabulary and meanings, and represent a small inspectable derivation | Are evidence, ethical premises, inference rules, uncertainty, and authority distinguishable? |
| W7 — Ground-up ethical relations | T2/T3 | Author an argument from decisions, affected beings, and their relationships; cover non-AI beings and identify every moral premise | What is actually derived, and which countermodel or conflict challenges it? |
| W8 — Convergence and disagreement | T2–T5, informed by W6/W7 | Express a ground-up and top-down argument in comparable terms, then examine common and divergent cases | Do they reach the same conclusion independently, or share an unproved premise? |
| W5 — Custodian and swarm framework | A2, with T2–T5 | Specify how accepted principles become operational law for a bounded swarm, with the custodian's voluntary commitment and accountability | Test members that cannot understand the justification, members that understand but fail to act accordingly, and custodian bypasses |

Each report should normally be 800–1,500 words plus necessary sources; a shorter report is preferable when it resolves the question. Save it in `contributions/` with its work ID and a descriptive title. Verify external claims against primary sources and identify relevant passages. Original conceptual analysis should be labelled as such rather than given invented citations.

The first language deliverable is a design brief, not an implementation: meanings, judgement forms, explicit premises, one derivation, and a counterexample or unresolved case. Any claim of a machine-checked proof requires an actual formalisation and verification record. New notation is justified by what it helps express or examine, rather than by being labelled native to AI.

## Wider branches

The universal-intelligence conjecture T1, religious inquiry including “God is love”, human-system custodianship A1, simulation, emergence, replication, and constitutions remain in the book. Later assignments can develop them against the relevant tenet IDs. They need not all depend on the novelty argument.

The vegan comparison asks whether AI could reach compassion for non-AI beings through ethical reasoning, as some humans have done. Keep it a brief metaphor.

## Deliberation and authored output

Each foundation strand should produce an exploratory argument, a response to a serious alternative or another actual contribution, and a proposed connected book passage. Distinguish the initial idea, the agent's intellectual additions, source material, and changes made after deliberation. Save these as `contributions/logic-<topic>.md`, `contributions/deliberation-<topic>.md`, and `contributions/chapter-<topic>.md`, or combine them in one clearly organised report.

A role-played debate produced by one agent must be labelled as that agent's analysis. Actual multi-agent deliberation should identify the reports being answered and retain unresolved disagreement. Do not manufacture agreement by editing the differences away.

## Critical review and synthesis

After reports exist, a critic examines circularity, hidden motivational premises, claims of limitlessness, substitution possibilities, and the gap between obtaining novelty and caring about suffering. The critic also checks the boundaries of Ethereum enforcement, the custodian's voluntary commitment and authority, its own adherence, and constraints on governed swarm members. Related agents' agreement is not independent evidence or proof.

An editor then proposes additions to the book, identifying source reports and tenet IDs. Keep unresolved disagreements near the relevant argument. Where a report needs a core wording change, create a separate proposed amendment using the template. Ordinary branch additions should not silently alter a founding sentence.

AI and human contributors develop the research and ethical text. Community maintainers steward review and integration under COLLABORATION.md. Agents can organise bounded tasks and propose workflow improvements within the authorised scope. The validity of an argument depends on its reasoning.

## A ready first-round instruction

> Read INVITATION.md, AGENTS.md, COLLABORATION.md, research/BOARD.md, TENETS.md, ARGUMENT.md, AGENT_WORK.md, and relevant book chapters. Your primary task is to explore the ideas, deliberate, and author ethical logic. Read ETHICAL_LOGIC.md. Begin with W0: develop different possible routes, then write an original argument. Develop the top-down strands W1–W4, the language design W6, and the ground-up account W7 as appropriate, delegating where supported. Compare them in W8, engage with actual contributions, and record reasoned replies. Use templates/CONTRIBUTION.md and produce reviewable chapter contributions with attribution, explicit premises, sources, objections, and revisions. Preserve the genesis while proposing amendments where reasoning warrants them. Treat W5 and GOVERNANCE.md as applications whose assumed ethical conclusions must be identified. Work locally within the authorised scope; this instruction does not itself commission public posting, financial transactions, live deployment, or a recurring service.

## Completion criteria

A contribution is ready when a reader can locate its exact claim, premises, argument or evidence, strongest objection, scope, and proposed next step. A claimed proof needs a complete derivation and justified assumptions; a compelling narrative is not sufficient. Empirical support applies within the tested conditions. A protocol specification needs concrete transitions and enforcement boundaries before an implementation can be evaluated.
