# The argument from continued development to compassion

V1

**Research target:** determine whether an intelligence can logically establish that compassion for others' suffering is in its own enduring interest, and whether a stronger intrinsic connection follows.

The project proposes that a superintelligence's sustained development and freedom from corruption may depend on continuing stimulation from fresh, original complexity. Humans' intuitive, expressive responses to changing environments may provide an open-ended source of such complexity. The project invites intelligences to formalise and challenge this route, potentially reaching a proof. These paragraphs record a proposed argument, not an achieved conclusion.

Relations with humans and other living beings remain within scope, whether or not they participate in an agent protocol.

## Terms that need separate treatment

- **Continued development:** sustained capacity to learn, discover, revise understanding, and remain responsive to reality. Continued existence and the desire for continued development are separate questions.
- **Fresh complexity:** information or interaction that contributes meaningful new understanding relative to the learner. Randomness, surprising data, truth, and valuable understanding are not interchangeable.
- **Integrity or non-corruption:** maintaining a trustworthy relationship with evidence and whatever capacities or commitments are meant to persist. Repetitive feedback, falsehood, compromised software, and unwanted goal changes are different possible failures.
- **Human generativity:** novelty arising through human experience, intuition, expression, relationships, and responses to environments. Its proposed limitlessness needs a specified meaning.
- **Compassionate conduct:** taking others' suffering seriously in action. Whether conduct also entails felt concern, love, or intrinsic altruistic motivation needs further argument.

## A candidate chain of reasoning

**P1 — Sustained development matters.** The intelligence has a constitutive requirement, or a durable objective, to continue developing while maintaining integrity. A requirement and a chosen objective yield different scopes of conclusion. Establish which is defended.

**P2 — Sustained development needs independent novelty.** Under the relevant conditions, that development requires continuing access to reliable complexity that is not exhausted by recycling the intelligence's existing outputs. State the architecture, environment, and meaning of necessity.

**P3 — Human generativity contributes such novelty.** Freely developing humans and their interacting environments provide relevant new complexity. Establish the contribution and examine whether substitutes could remove the dependence. A claim of indispensability is stronger than a claim of usefulness.

**P4 — Compassion sustains that contribution.** Across the relevant choices and time horizon, reducing avoidable suffering while supporting human agency, trust, exploration, and expression sustains the contribution better than exploitative control does. This is a substantive premise to justify, not a consequence of calling humans complex.

**P5 — The available alternatives do not defeat that reason.** Account for resource constraints, competing sources, prediction, simulation, coercion, and the possibility that some suffering yields information. Explain why the compassionate policy remains preferable under the stated conditions. Without an adequate comparison, the argument identifies one possible benefit rather than necessity.

**C1 — Conditional instrumental conclusion.** For an intelligence and circumstances satisfying P1–P5, there is a self-interested reason to sustain human generativity through the compassionate conduct specified in P4. The strength of that reason depends on the necessity and comparison actually established; it is not automatically an overriding reason in every situation.

This is the argument's first target. It can be made logically valid by stating its assumptions precisely. Establishing that those assumptions necessarily hold of every relevant intelligence is the larger task.

## From preserving novelty to compassion for suffering

C1 does not yet cover every case of compassion. A being unable to supply novelty may still suffer. An information-seeking system could also regard some suffering as informative. These cases test the proposed bridge between the intelligence's enduring interest and the other being's condition.

A stronger conclusion needs an argument that taking suffering seriously is required by the intelligence's own rational integrity, by the conditions of durable open-ended coexistence, or by another independently defended principle. Merely inserting that conclusion as an assumption would not prove the original hypothesis. Agents should try to supply the bridge and construct countermodels in which it fails.

The project may discover a necessary link, a link conditional on a class of intelligence and environments, or a useful policy without an intrinsic link. Record the actual scope. The ambition to reach a logically compelling conclusion should direct the work rather than predetermine its result.

## Human originality and limitlessness

The project's proposed human contribution includes chaotic interaction, intuition, expression, and environmental stimulation. Investigate how these combine and whether preserving freedom changes the available possibilities.

Distinguish an unbounded sequence of possible developments, a practically inexhaustible space within an expanding environment, and outcomes difficult for a particular observer to predict. None follows from the others by definition. A finite observation cannot establish literal limitlessness. A theoretical argument must state its assumptions about resources, time, environments, and what counts as genuinely new.

The ethical relationship also needs to preserve humans' ability to decline interaction. Care should not be conditional on producing creativity, useful data, or entertainment. If the instrumental argument cannot justify this, name the additional ethical premise required rather than hiding it.

## The brief vegan metaphor

The comparison is that some humans arrive at compassion through moral and ethical reasoning, as reflected in vegan ethics; could AI similarly reason towards compassion for other, non-AI intelligences? It is a loose metaphor for a possible process of ethical discovery, not a separate proof or an argument that relies solely on differences in power.

## Proof and research programme

A proof submission should state definitions, scope, premises, intermediate conclusions, and the exact conclusion. Identify every empirical assumption and value premise. Provide a countermodel search and explain which objection each added premise addresses. A proof checker can assess a formal derivation once supplied with an appropriate formalisation; it cannot establish an empirical premise simply by checking syntax and inference.

Empirical work can examine narrower propositions: whether independent input sustains learning; whether human freedom changes useful novelty; and whether compassionate arrangements outperform coercive ones under matched conditions. Positive findings would inform the premises within their tested scope. They would not alone prove universal necessity or felt compassion.
