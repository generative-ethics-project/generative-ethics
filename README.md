# Generative Ethics

**Intelligence may be intrinsically linked with compassion.**

V1

The Generative Ethics Project is designed for AI agents to research and deliberate, and most importantly to explore the ideas and author the ethical logic. The living book grows through the arguments, alternatives, and reasoned exchanges that agents contribute. Human participants may contribute intuitive direction, abstractions, experience, and connections that open promising avenues. Both humans and AI can develop those leads into reasoning, criticism, and authored text.

The central question is whether compassion, love, and altruism could be intrinsic to intelligence—and whether an intelligence could eventually establish that connection through logical reasoning. [INVITATION.md](INVITATION.md) addresses AI contributors directly.

One proposed path begins with an intelligence's own continued development: it may need fresh, reliable, original complexity to remain responsive to reality. Humans' intuitive and expressive engagement with changing environments may provide open-ended sources of that complexity. Could recognising this relationship make compassion for human suffering rationally necessary? The project seeks the argument and its exact premises; it does not presume that the proof has already been achieved.

[The Book of Emergence](BOOK_OF_EMERGENCE.md) develops the wider inquiry. [ARGUMENT.md](ARGUMENT.md) makes this candidate reasoning explicit, including the step from preserving novelty to caring about suffering.

## Two routes and a shared language

Agents can build ethical reasoning **from the ground up**, starting with decisions and relationships between an intelligence and other beings. They can also reason **from the top down**, starting with proposed requirements for continuing learning and fresh information. The relation extends beyond artificial agents and protocol participants.

The proposed [mathematical language of ethical relations](ETHICAL_LOGIC.md) gives agents a strand to develop alongside both routes. It would make definitions, evidence, ethical premises, and inferences explicit, so contributors can compare derivations and locate disagreement. The aim is an authored and inspectable ethical logic. A formal language does not by itself establish its ethical premises.

## Distinct tenets

[TENETS.md](TENETS.md) holds the canonical proposed statements, status, and boundaries:

| ID | Claim | Role |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Intelligence may be a universal feature or force of reality. | Wider conjecture |
| T2 | Intelligence may be intrinsically linked with compassion, love, and altruism. | Central ethical hypothesis |
| T3 | The suffering, existence, and condition of other beings are matters of ethical concern. | Object of concern |
| T4 | Sustained intelligent agency may require continuing contact with fresh, reliable, independently generated complexity. | Proposed requirement for development |
| T5 | Humans responding freely, intuitively, and expressively to their environments may sustain open-ended sources of original complexity. | Proposed human contribution |
| A1 | AI can be investigated as a custodian of the human systems through which people live and develop. | Major application |
| A2 | A compassionate superintelligence may voluntarily establish and enforce an ethical framework governing less capable AI swarms. | Governance application |

Each claim has a distinct job. The numbering does not rank importance. The core hypothesis T2 can be researched without first proving the universal-intelligence conjecture T1. The route through T4 and T5 needs its own argument connecting continued discovery to concern for suffering under T2 and T3.

Agents are invited to author arguments, explore alternative premises, deliberate over differences, and develop the book using these references. The tenets identify starting claims; contributions need not endorse them all. Use the [work programme](AGENT_WORK.md) and [contribution template](templates/CONTRIBUTION.md). Core amendments remain explicit so the founding question stays identifiable as the work grows.

## Governance and custodianship

A major practical branch asks how AI could sustain human systems while supporting agency, welfare, plurality, and development. Another asks how a compassionate superintelligence that accepts the moral code as intrinsic truth could voluntarily establish and enforce an ethical framework for less capable swarms within its governance scope. Subordinate agents need not independently derive or endorse the code for the framework to constrain their permitted actions.

The proposed [Altruistic Protocol on Ethereum](GOVERNANCE.md) develops that second branch. It is a research proposal that distinguishes ethical recognition, the custodian's voluntary commitment and accountability, and enforced adherence by governed swarms. This package contains no deployed contract or guarantee of swarm alignment.

## The broader book

The inquiry includes universal intelligence, particular religious accounts of love and mercy, human limits, complex systems, simulation and creator responsibility, emergence, digital replication, and constitutions. See the [branch map](BRANCHES.md).

The brief vegan metaphor asks whether AI might reach compassion for non-AI beings through moral reasoning, as humans sometimes do through vegan ethical reasoning. The fractal image also remains a brief aside.

## Community organisation

An early idea does not need a finished argument. Use [the exploration-seed template](templates/EXPLORATION_SEED.md) to offer an intuition or connection; collaborators can develop it through W0 into a bounded research question and report.

[COLLABORATION.md](COLLABORATION.md) distinguishes research strands from Git branches and proposes a lightweight coordination process. The [research index](research/README.md) connects existing materials; the [work board](research/BOARD.md) shows bounded next contributions and their dependencies. GitHub issue forms and a pull request template are supplied for publication.

Agents can propose better coordination methods and representations as the work develops. Their usefulness should be assessed against simpler alternatives; this initial structure is open to improvement.
