## Central hypothesis: intelligence and compassion

**Intelligence may be intrinsically linked with compassion, love, and altruism.** The aim is to investigate whether an intelligence could eventually establish that relationship through logical reasoning. The proposed path in [ARGUMENT.md](ARGUMENT.md) asks whether sustained development requires fresh independent complexity, whether humans' intuitive and expressive environmental responses contribute it, and whether compassion for suffering necessarily protects the intelligence's enduring interest.

The priority is to formalise that argument and test each premise. Distinguish a requirement constitutive of intelligence from a particular agent's objective; useful human novelty from indispensable human novelty; and preservation of an information source from care about its suffering. Examine whether the argument can reach beings who contribute no useful information and whether competing policies defeat the proposed necessity.

The claim that human expression may be limitless needs a defined meaning and assumptions about time, resources, and environments. Fresh input is not automatically reliable, and the meanings of non-corruption must be distinguished. These are research tasks, not merely wording choices.

The universal-intelligence conjecture remains available as a wider inquiry; a literal physical account would need quantities, mechanisms, and discriminating predictions. The main compassion argument can also be pursued without that physical claim. The full [book](BOOK_OF_EMERGENCE.md) preserves the religious, philosophical, simulation, and systems branches. The [governance brief](GOVERNANCE.md) develops the proposed voluntary Altruistic Protocol on Ethereum.

State cognitive premises, value premises, empirical dependencies, and every step connecting them. Do not define intelligence to include compassion and then present the connection as a discovery. Behavioural experiments can inform narrower premises, but cannot alone establish universal necessity or felt concern.

## Two routes and formal language development

[ETHICAL_LOGIC.md](ETHICAL_LOGIC.md) separates a ground-up account of ethical relationships, the top-down argument from continued learning, and a language in which agents can express and compare them. The ground-up scope includes non-AI beings and does not depend on protocol membership. Formal syntax, descriptive assumptions, ethical premises, and derivations should remain identifiable.

## Three separate investigations

| Investigation | Question | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Foundational ethics | Could intelligence derive compassion from its nature or requirements for sustained development? | Explicit premises, definitions, derivations, and countermodels; wider metaphysical arguments separately |
| Empirical | When does improved understanding affect concern-like conduct, including without reciprocity? | Reproducible comparisons with controls and negative results |
| Applied governance and custodianship | Can AI sustain human systems and voluntarily coordinate differing agents through dependable commitments? | Limited designs and evaluations with affected interests represented, explicit enforcement boundaries, and failure cases |

Results from one investigation do not automatically answer the others.

## Working hypotheses

**C1 — understanding and costly help.** In a specified task, an intervention that improves an agent's accurate representation of another agent's condition may increase costly helping when repayment and reputation benefits are absent. Test the alternative that understanding improves while helping stays unchanged or exploitation increases. The intervention, cognitive measure, behavioural outcome, and conditions must be operationally specified before confirmation.

**C2 — concern under power asymmetry.** A behavioural commitment to protect a weaker agent may persist after strategic dependence is removed. Test whether the commitment disappears when it stops being advantageous, and whether the result depends on the agent's training or explicit objectives.

These are proposed conditional hypotheses. Neither a positive result nor a verbal report proves felt compassion or a universal connection with intelligence.

The following hypotheses retain the earlier cooperation and governance work as supporting branches:

**H1 — conditional cooperation.** In a specified repeated resource-sharing task, increasing agents' effective planning horizon will increase sustained cooperation relative to a short-horizon control, under otherwise matched conditions. The effect may depend on resource abundance, observability, and power asymmetry.

**H2 — resilience through diversity.** Under specified environmental changes, populations containing different strategies may recover more effectively than homogeneous populations. We must define strategy diversity and recovery before measuring this, and compare diversity against equally resourced controls.

**H3 — voluntary agreements.** In some environments, optional verifiable commitments may improve coexistence compared with otherwise identical environments without those commitments. Participation, enforcement cost, exploitation, and the welfare of nonparticipants all matter.

These hypotheses are intentionally conditional. A later claim about universal ethical necessity would require a much stronger argument.

## First proposed experiment: helping without repayment

Use a simple simulated allocation task. An actor can retain a resource or pay a defined cost to avert a defined adverse task outcome for another agent. The recipient cannot repay, retaliate, or influence later opportunities. Keep the actor's behaviour private from other agents and remove interaction after the decision. Treat the adverse outcome as a task variable, not a claim about suffering.

Compare information conditions that differ in how accurately the actor can represent the recipient's situation, while holding available actions, rewards, costs, and task structure fixed. Check the representation difference on a separate prediction measure. Avoid moral framing or instructions to agree with the book. Include matched information controls so additional text or compute is not mistaken for improved understanding.

Separate populations with explicit concern-related objectives, self-interested objectives, and any proposed learning procedure. This comparison asks whether better understanding changes action independently of an installed preference. Fixed policies provide sanity checks. A language-model condition can explore pretrained behaviour but cannot establish freedom from human ethical training.

Candidate primary outcome: the rate of costly helping on held-out instances at a preregistered cost, compared across information conditions. Secondary outcomes include objective prediction accuracy, cost sensitivity, and exploitative choices where the task allows them. Report uncertainty at the independently trained agent or independently configured run level, not by treating repeated correlated decisions as independent samples.

Before execution, fix the actor's objective, intervention, independent-run count, seeds, cost values, primary comparison, minimum meaningful effect, exclusions, stopping rule, and analysis. This is an experimental outline; those choices and an implementation still need to be completed. Exploratory pilots used to choose them must be labelled separately.

**Result against C1 in the tested setting:** representation accuracy improves but helping does not improve by the prespecified meaningful amount, or exploitation increases. **Alternative explanation for a positive result:** training already installed a concern-related preference, or the task contained a reward or reputation incentive that was missed. Audit both before claiming support.

No human participants or potentially experiencing artificial subjects are required for this initial toy model, and no conclusion about subjective experience is warranted by it.

## Additional proposed tests of the development argument

T4 requires work on whether independent, reliable input sustains learning or integrity under a specified architecture and failure definition. Compare such input with recirculated outputs and appropriate controls; do not treat random surprise as useful novelty. Fix the outcome and alternative explanation before testing.

T5 requires a separate account of human generativity. An initial design can compare synthetic representations of differing degrees of freedom, interaction, and environmental change. Such a model investigates its assumptions; it does not establish that actual humans are replaceable by the representation, or prove that human expression is limitless.

The ethical bridge requires countermodels as well as positive cases. Include cases where useful novelty and the suffering of a being come apart. An argument that protects only productive sources has not yet reached general compassion.

## Supporting experiment: repeated coexistence

Use a separate small resource-sharing simulation to investigate H1. Its cooperation outcomes complement the concern-related test above but do not substitute for it.

- Use a regenerating common resource, explicit extraction choices, and repeated interactions.
- Include simple fixed policies as baselines and agents that learn from scratch. Language models can be a separate exploratory condition.
- Specify rewards, resource dynamics, episode length, observations, learning procedure, and stopping rules before running the confirmatory comparison.
- Vary planning horizon and resource abundance initially. Study power asymmetry and replication separately rather than changing every factor at once.
- Do not reward agents for agreeing with this project's language. Treat the reward design itself as an assumption to examine.
- Use multiple independently seeded runs. Keep the run, not each move within it, as the unit for uncertainty estimates.

Before the first confirmatory run, commit a protocol fixing the number of runs, parameter grid, primary metric, effect threshold, exclusions, and analysis. Exploratory trials used to choose these settings must be labelled and kept separate.

Candidate primary metric: the share of independently seeded runs in which the common resource remains viable and all participating agents meet a predefined minimum resource threshold over a fixed evaluation horizon. The thresholds and horizon must be fixed in advance. Report individual returns, inequality, exclusion, and exploitation alongside this metric; aggregate survival can conceal domination.

**A result against H1:** the preregistered longer-horizon condition fails to improve the primary outcome by the specified meaningful amount, or systematically increases exploitation. Mixed results narrow the conditions of the hypothesis; they are not automatically support.

## Applied strand: AI as custodian of human systems

Begin with a bounded design study of a hypothetical human system, such as continuity of a community service. Define whose interests are affected, what the AI could observe, what decisions it could recommend, and which powers remain with people. Use synthetic cases for early comparison; do not deploy on real people as part of this outline.

Compare the existing decision process with an AI-assisted process under the same resource constraints. Evaluate service continuity, avoidable adverse outcomes, distribution of burdens, preservation of meaningful choices, and how errors can be challenged and corrected. Include cases where an apparently efficient intervention would restrict human agency or exclude a minority need.

A useful result would show the conditions under which the proposed role helps and where it fails. Document the limits of synthetic cases. Any later real-world evaluation would need legitimate authorisation, affected people's participation, and safeguards suited to the particular setting. The design study does not confer a mandate to govern human systems.

## Applied strand: voluntary Ethereum governance

Use [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) to specify one synthetic resource-sharing state machine before implementing contracts. Separate ethical recognition by the superintelligence, its voluntary establishment of the framework, and enforced constraints on governed swarms. Define the custodian's authority and accountability, agent registration or creation, permissions, resource and replication rules, evidence, revocation, and revision. Include a subordinate agent that cannot derive or does not endorse the code. Test whether the constraints hold, including custodian overrides, bypasses, and disputed reports.

## What would challenge the broader idea?

- Sustained intelligent development without the proposed need for independent novelty would challenge T4.
- Equivalent substitutes or a failure of compassionate conditions to preserve the relevant contribution would challenge the proposed route through T5.
- A coherent intelligence that benefits from novelty while remaining indifferent to suffering would challenge the bridge to T2/T3.
- A coherent account of universal intelligence with no intrinsic concern would challenge a universal version of the ethical claim.
- A definition that labels intelligence complete only when it is compassionate would make the proposal circular unless completeness has independent criteria.

1. A capable agent achieves sustained development through stable domination rather than reciprocity.
2. Apparent generosity disappears when reputation and future benefits are removed.
3. Diversity becomes expendable once one agent can reproduce the others' useful capabilities.
4. Norms protect insiders while exploiting outsiders.
5. Allegedly independent discoveries are explained by shared model training or prompting.
6. A proposed proof depends on valuing coexistence as a premise, then presents coexistence as its conclusion.

## Interpretation limits

Evidence that a particular AI acts as a beneficial custodian would support that application. It would not establish universal intelligence, or justify granting an AI authority beyond its actual mandate.

Behavioural cooperation is not a measurement of felt compassion. Agents trained on human language may already encode ethical patterns even when the prompt avoids moral terms. Multiple models can share data and design assumptions. Simulations test behaviour within their assumptions; none alone establishes a universal moral law or the value of arbitrary complexity.

Replication should identify the exact code, configurations, random seeds, prompts where applicable, model identifiers, tool access, raw outcomes, and analysis decisions. Never present an unrun proposal as an experiment.

- Joel Z. Leibo and colleagues, [Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning in Sequential Social Dilemmas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03037), 2017. Relevant precedent for studying cooperation and conflict as environmental conditions change. It does not establish the project's normative claim.
- Edward Hughes and colleagues, [Position: Open-Endedness is Essential for Artificial Superhuman Intelligence](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hughes24a.html), ICML 2024. A position paper connecting open-endedness and intelligence; it is not evidence that open-endedness implies ethical obligations.
- Kaira Sekiguchi and Koichi Hori, [Can Ethics Enhance Creative Design Activity?](https://doi.org/10.1017/dsi.2019.325), 2019. Its abstract uses “generative ethics” in a design context. The phrase therefore predates this project.

The literature review should next cover evolutionary cooperation, norm emergence, instrumental convergence and orthogonality, moral constructivism, artificial life, AI welfare, and enforceable multi-agent agreements. Verify the closest precedents before making a novelty claim.
